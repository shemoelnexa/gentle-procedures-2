The first days at home

A snug swaddle keeps your son settled in the first day or two, mostly because it stills the startle reflex that keeps newborns jumping awake. The trick is where the snugness goes. Wrap him comfortably around the arms and chest, but always leave his hips and legs room to bend up and out, never straightened or pressed together. His hips need that freedom to develop properly, so think firm up top, relaxed below. Many babies protest the wrap at first, and most settle beautifully once they’re snug. Always lay him on his back to sleep, and keep him comfortably cool rather than warm. We’ll show you the exact technique before you leave, and send a short video home with you.

Skin-to-skin is the most powerful settling tool you have. Held bare against a parent’s chest, a newborn’s heart rate, breathing and temperature all steady, stress hormones fall, and calming hormones rise in both of you. It works just as well with dads. The bare chest of a calm father does exactly the same job, so it’s a real chance for him to hold his son close while mum rests. Time it for the unsettled times, especially the first evening as the numbing eases. Keep it safe and simple: your son upright and chest to chest, his face turned to the side and clearly visible, nose and mouth uncovered, and you awake and attentive throughout. Ten unhurried minutes can often settle him completely.

When your son is unsettled, often a few hours later as the numbing eases, this hold gives him the secure, slightly curled feeling of the womb. Sitting down, fold his arms gently across his chest and hold them there under the flat of one hand, with that same hand steadying his chin so his head is supported and his face stays clear. Slip your other hand under his bottom to take his weight, and let his legs dangle naturally. Tilt him gently forward, away from you, to about forty-five degrees, so he rests securely against your hands. Then add a soft rock, a gentle sway or the lightest bounce, and keep your own breathing slow and your voice low. A calm, settled parent helps a baby settle too. You’ll know it’s working when the tension drains out of him, his fists relax open and he softens into your hands. Give it a minute or two, as settling takes a little time. If your arm tires, pass him to your partner in the same hold. Our nurses will place your hands for you before you leave, so you already know the feel of it.

We’ll always show you, explain it calmly, and decide together what, if anything, you’d like to do. If we ever spot a more significant difference, we’ll pause and guide you to the right specialist, so he has every option open to him.

Take the worry in its two halves. How it will look: for the great majority of boys, a newborn circumcision done gently and healed well settles into a neat, natural appearance. Most of the visible settling happens in the first weeks, and the rest follows quietly over the months after, with nothing for you to watch or correct. Some normal variation is just that, normal. No two are identical, and a little different is not the same as a problem. How it will work: he will use the bathroom normally, and as a grown man he can expect normal sexual function and to father children of his own. The calm behind these answers comes from experience, not a brochure. This is the one procedure we do, over and over, and we’ve watched time after time how predictably babies settle. He won’t remember any of it. He’ll simply grow into himself.

Cost, insurance and practical questions

Costs in Dubai vary from provider to provider, and the number on its own tells you very little. Here is a list of questions worth asking anywhere. Who will actually perform the procedure, and how much of their work is this? Will you meet them before anything happens? How is your baby’s comfort managed, what anaesthetic is used, and how is it given? Can you stay with him? What happens after you leave, what written guidance, what follow-up, and who do you contact with a worry? And what does the fee include, completely?

Our answer to that last question is simple. One fee covers everything: your son’s procedure with the Pollock Technique, the whole of your family’s visit, the discharge pack, the day-four photograph review, and the care that follows at home. If we do it, it’s included. Nothing is added on the day, and we only take payment once we’re all set to go ahead. If the appointment ever needs to move, or your son is referred onward for specialist care, you’re never out of pocket. The fee itself is on our pricing page, in plain sight.

It depends on your policy. When circumcision is chosen for religious, cultural or personal reasons, as it is for most of the families we welcome, UAE insurers generally class it as an elective procedure, and most policies don’t cover it. Where there’s a specific medical reason, some policies do, so it’s worth checking directly with your insurer before the day. We don’t bill insurers directly. Your fee is settled with us at the clinic, and if your policy allows you to claim it back, we’ll gladly prepare everything you need: a receipt, a procedure summary, and any paperwork your insurer asks for. Whatever your policy says, our single fee means no surprises on our side. You’ll know exactly where you stand before the day.
