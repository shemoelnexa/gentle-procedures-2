Preparing Your Home for the First Week

By The Gentle Procedures Team·Six small preparations

The procedure takes about a minute. The week that follows happens at home — and a little preparation before the day makes it a calm one. Here's what families tell us actually helped.

Six preparations

01 The changing table 02 The first evening 03 The fridge sheet 04 The household 05 A quiet corner 06 Keeping us close

01

Stock the changing table.

Vaseline is the hero of the fortnight — have more than you think you need, somewhere you can reach it one-handed. Plenty of nappies, and whatever you already use for gentle cleaning. That's genuinely the list; healing asks for consistency, not equipment.

02

Plan the first evening before it arrives.

It is often the least settled part of the whole fortnight — the numbing fades, and many babies have a harder evening. Decide in advance: dim lights, no visitors, feeds on demand, and which parent is on the first shift. An evening you've planned for feels completely different from one that surprises you.

03

Put the checklist on the fridge.

Our First Week sheet lives on the fridge for a reason — at 3am nobody wants to search an email. Print it before the day; it pairs with the full guidance in your discharge pack.

04

Brief the rest of the household.

Grandparents and helpers do better with one clear line: lots of Vaseline, daily bath once we start, and call the clinic rather than Google. And if there's a big brother or sister, give them a job — chief fetcher of nappies is a real position with real prestige.

05

Protect a quiet corner.

A comfortable feeding spot with water and a phone charger within reach will be the most-used square metre in the house. The first feed after the procedure is often the most settling thing of all — and the hundred feeds after it deserve a good chair.

06

And keep us close.

Save our number before the day. We check in with you anyway — but the whole point of the first week is that you're never doing it alone.
